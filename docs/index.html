The corner most tools get wrong

A 45° corner is a closed box, not two panels and a hope. Panelwright builds both return sides, a pentagon worktop cut to the mitre, a three-piece plinth and five legs — with the diagonal door hung outside the carcass so it clears.

The blind L corner gets the same treatment, including the dead space you have to reach past.

CNC and beam saw

Round parts are round

Most cut lists see a bounding box. Panelwright keeps the real contour — which changes what the router cuts and what the client is billed.

Where the money hides

A Ø1260 tabletop has 3.96 m of edge. A bounding box says 5.04 m. Every round part you have ever quoted carried that difference.

The nesting view draws the true circle, the DXF exports the contour on its own layer with the blank on BLANK, and small parts drop into the corners of the blank when the shop cuts on a router.

FAQ

Questions shops ask before they buy

Is this really a whole system, or a plugin with extras?

It covers design, production files, your own databases, purchasing and stock, client paperwork, the project archive and profit per job — all from one calculation. It is not a factory MRP: no machine scheduling, no accounting integration, no multi-site server. Within a one- to twenty-person shop, it goes end to end.

Does it work with my own drawn cabinets, not just the generator?

Yes. Any group or component is read as a part. Panelwright takes thickness, size, material and exposed edges from the geometry itself, so a model you drew by hand works the same as one the generator built.

Which SketchUp versions?

SketchUp 2021 and newer, on Windows and macOS. It runs inside SketchUp — there is nothing else to install.

Can two or three designers use it together?

Yes. Point everyone at one shared folder on a network drive or a synced folder. File locking, atomic writes and re-read-merge mean nobody's price change or archive entry gets silently overwritten.

What happens to my data if I stop paying, or change computers?

Your projects, prices and settings are files on your own computer and stay there. A key just unlocks the software again. Moving machines is one backup file.

Is the nesting good enough for CNC?

Yes, with one caveat worth knowing. Nesting is MaxRects with kerf, margins, grain and offcut re-use, and round or shaped parts keep their true contour in the DXF. Small parts are placed into the corners of round blanks — useful on a router, wrong on a panel saw, so there is a setting and a warning.

Can I use my own hardware, not the catalogue brands?

Yes. The catalogue ships with real Blum, Hettich, Häfele, Grass and Salice models and their dimensions and drilling patterns, and you can add your own alongside them.

What does the trial include?

Everything, for ten days. No card, no watermark, no limit on cabinets or exports. Your projects and settings stay after it ends — a key unlocks them again.
